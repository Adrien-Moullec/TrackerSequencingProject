INTRODUCTION

Music track sequencing was used in older game consoles where storage space and audio capabilities were limited. Music used to be a list of text commands that played preset instrumental tones. Audio has since been upgraded to pre-recorded tracks, creating greater immersive experiences.

This research dives into the history of music in gaming and explores the question “Can track sequencers be relevant in modern game development?”.


OBJECTIVES

•Demonstrate the potential of legacy music playback formats in modern systems. 
•Allow quick prototype or full music production for a game developer.
•Removes the barrier between level design/gameplay programming and music creation.
•Allow for future modification of tracks due to the text-command style of music files such as transformative or generative music.


METHODOLOGY

The first step is creating an sf2 file playback method, using TinySoundFont in C, that can be read by other applications. A sequence editor tool !!!!!!! will be created to create and adjust musical files.

Samples of tracker sequence playback will be implemented in a Unity project for game testers to play. An accompanying questionnaire will provide input on the authenticity and immersion of the music. 

The results of these playtest will allow insight into the direction and potential that this type of music format holds.
